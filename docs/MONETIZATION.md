- triage of findings and false positives
- remediation guidance
- team rollout and developer onboarding
- ongoing security reporting and support

## 3. Free-to-paid conversion

### Community → Solo
Trigger: developer wants AI/remediation without supplying and maintaining their own provider key.

### Solo → Pro
Trigger: multiple repositories, need for centralized findings and historical analytics.

### Pro → Team
Trigger: shared rules, auditability, organization policy and multiple developers.

### Team → Business
Trigger: compliance, onboarding, higher limits, reporting and priority support.

## 4. Acquisition engine

The public Action should be the top of the funnel:

1. Publish v1.4.0 to GitHub Marketplace.
2. Make installation take less than five minutes.
3. Add a clear "Need us to configure it for you?" commercial call-to-action.
4. Recruit the first 25 design partners through developer communities and direct outreach.
5. Convert successful installs into case studies and testimonials.
6. Grow toward the GitHub Marketplace paid-app installation threshold.
7. Launch the managed GitHub App after organization verification and financial onboarding.

## 7. Marketplace path

GitHub Marketplace paid plans apply to GitHub Apps rather than a standalone Action. The paid App must be owned by a verified organization, implement Marketplace purchase lifecycle events, support monthly and annual plans, complete financial onboarding, and meet GitHub's current eligibility requirements.

Until that threshold is met, the free Action plus paid implementation/retainer services is the revenue engine.
